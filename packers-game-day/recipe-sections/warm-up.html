Brandy Old-Fashioned Glazed MeatballsOpen Recipe ↓
Serves3 people • about 15 small meatballs
Total Time45 minutes
DifficultyEasy–Moderate
EquipmentSheet pan or smoker-safe pan; medium skillet/saucepan; instant-read thermometer helpful.
Make-ahead: Shape and bake/smoke the meatballs up to 1 day ahead. Rewarm them directly in the glaze.

Ingredients

  • 1 lb 80/20 ground beef
  • 1/3 cup plain breadcrumbs
  • 1 large egg
  • 2 tbsp finely grated or minced onion
  • 1 tbsp Worcestershire sauce
  • 1 tsp kosher salt
  • 1/2 tsp black pepper
  • 1/2 tsp garlic powder
  • 1 tbsp neutral oil, if baking
  • 2 tbsp butter
  • 1/3 cup brandy
  • 1/3 cup packed brown sugar
  • 1/4 cup beef broth
  • 1 1/2 tbsp Worcestershire sauce
  • 1 tbsp orange marmalade or 2 tsp orange juice concentrate (optional but very Old-Fashioned-ish)
  • 4 dashes aromatic bitters
  • 1 tsp Dijon mustard
  • Pinch of black pepper

Step-by-step

  1. Heat the oven to 425°F, or set the smoker to 250°F. Line a sheet pan if baking.
  2. In a bowl, combine beef, breadcrumbs, egg, onion, Worcestershire, salt, pepper and garlic powder. Mix just until evenly combined; overmixing makes meatballs dense.
  3. Roll into 15 small meatballs, roughly 1 heaping tablespoon each. For the oven, lightly oil the pan and bake 12–15 minutes. For the smoker, smoke 35–45 minutes. Either way, cook until the centers reach 160°F.
  4. While they cook, melt butter in a skillet over medium heat. Add brandy carefully and let it bubble 1–2 minutes. Stir in brown sugar, broth, Worcestershire, marmalade, bitters, Dijon and pepper.
  5. Simmer the glaze 6–8 minutes, stirring occasionally, until glossy and just thick enough to coat a spoon.
  6. Add the cooked meatballs to the glaze. Toss gently and simmer 3–5 minutes so the glaze clings to every meatball.
  7. Transfer to a small warm serving dish and spoon the extra glaze over the top. Serve with toothpicks.
KaySo note: For a stronger Wisconsin supper-club vibe, use Korbel-style brandy if you already have it. The glaze should be savory-sweet, not candy sweet; add a splash of beef broth if it gets too sticky.
Mini Wisconsin Hot Beef SundaesOpen Recipe ↓
Serves3 people • 6 appetizer cups
Total Time55 minutes
DifficultyModerate
EquipmentSaucepan; skillet; 6 small ramekins, espresso cups, or sturdy appetizer cups.
Make-ahead: Make the mashed potatoes, beef and gravy earlier in the day. Reheat separately and assemble right before serving.

Ingredients

  • 1 lb Yukon Gold potatoes, peeled and cut into chunks
  • 2 tbsp butter
  • 1/4 cup whole milk or half-and-half, warmed
  • 1/3 tsp kosher salt, plus more to taste
  • 8 oz shaved or very thinly sliced roast beef
  • 1 tbsp butter
  • 1/2 small yellow onion, thinly sliced
  • 1 tbsp flour
  • 1 cup beef broth
  • 1 tsp Worcestershire sauce
  • 1/2 tsp Dijon mustard
  • Black pepper to taste
  • 1/2 cup shredded sharp cheddar
  • 1/3 cup crispy fried onions

Step-by-step

  1. Put the potatoes in a saucepan, cover with cold salted water and bring to a boil. Reduce to a steady simmer and cook 12–15 minutes, until completely tender.
  2. Drain well, return the potatoes to the warm pan for 1 minute to steam off excess moisture, then mash with butter, warm milk and salt. Keep warm.
  3. Meanwhile, melt 1 tablespoon butter in a skillet over medium heat. Add onion and cook 8–10 minutes until soft and golden.
  4. Sprinkle flour over the onion and stir for 1 minute. Slowly whisk in beef broth, then add Worcestershire and Dijon. Simmer 4–6 minutes until a spoon dragged through the gravy leaves a brief trail.
  5. Tear or chop the roast beef into bite-size pieces and stir it into the gravy just long enough to heat through. Do not boil it aggressively or the beef can toughen.
  6. Spoon about 1/3 cup mashed potatoes into each small serving cup. Make a little dip in the center.
  7. Top each with beef and gravy, then cheddar and crispy onions. Serve warm with small forks.
KaySo note: These are intentionally small. The goal is a ridiculous two- or three-bite appetizer, not six bowls of mashed potatoes before dinner.
Smoked Buffalo Chicken PinwheelsOpen Recipe ↓
Serves3 people • 12 pinwheels
Total Time50 minutes
DifficultyEasy
EquipmentSheet pan; parchment; smoker or oven.
Make-ahead: Mix the filling and roll the pastry up to 6 hours ahead. Chill, then slice and bake/smoke when needed.

Ingredients

  • 1 sheet frozen puff pastry, thawed but still cold
  • 1 cup cooked shredded chicken
  • 1/3 cup buffalo sauce
  • 3 oz cream cheese, softened
  • 3/4 cup shredded sharp cheddar
  • 2 scallions, thinly sliced
  • 1/2 tsp ranch seasoning
  • 1 egg beaten with 1 tsp water
  • Ranch dressing, for serving

Step-by-step

  1. Heat the oven or smoker to 400°F. If using the smoker, make sure it is running clean smoke; puff pastry picks up smoke quickly.
  2. Mix chicken, buffalo sauce, cream cheese, cheddar, scallions and ranch seasoning until evenly combined.
  3. Unfold the puff pastry on a lightly floured surface. Gently roll it into roughly a 10x12-inch rectangle.
  4. Spread the chicken filling over the pastry, leaving a 1/2-inch border along one long edge.
  5. Starting from the opposite long edge, roll tightly into a log. Chill 10 minutes if the pastry has become soft.
  6. Slice into 12 equal rounds with a sharp knife. Arrange cut-side up on parchment and brush the exposed pastry lightly with egg wash.
  7. Bake or smoke 18–23 minutes, rotating the pan once, until deeply golden and crisp.
  8. Rest 5 minutes before serving. Put ranch on the side rather than drenching the pinwheels so the pastry stays crisp.
KaySo note: If your smoker struggles to stay at 400°F, smoke them at 325°F for about 10 minutes for flavor, then finish in a 425°F oven until crisp.
Mini Loaded Potato CupsOpen Recipe ↓
Serves3 people • 12 potato cups
Total Time55 minutes
DifficultyEasy
EquipmentSheet pan; small spoon or melon baller.
Make-ahead: Roast and scoop the potatoes a few hours ahead. Fill and finish just before serving.

Ingredients

  • 12 small baby Yukon Gold or red potatoes, about 1 1/2 inches wide
  • 1 tbsp olive oil
  • 3/4 tsp kosher salt, divided
  • 1/2 tsp black pepper
  • 4 slices bacon, cooked crisp and chopped
  • 3/4 cup shredded sharp cheddar
  • 1/3 cup sour cream
  • 2 scallions, thinly sliced
  • 1/4 tsp smoked paprika

Step-by-step

  1. Heat oven to 425°F. Toss potatoes with oil, 1/2 teaspoon salt and pepper. Roast 25–30 minutes until tender.
  2. Cool until you can handle them, then halve each potato lengthwise.
  3. Use a small spoon to scoop a shallow well from each half. Leave enough potato around the skin so the cups do not collapse.
  4. Arrange cut-side up on the sheet pan and return to the oven for 8–10 minutes so the edges dry and crisp.
  5. Mix cheddar, bacon and about half the scallions. Spoon a small mound into each potato cup.
  6. Bake another 5–7 minutes until the cheese is melted and bubbling.
  7. Stir sour cream with the remaining 1/4 teaspoon salt and smoked paprika. Add a tiny dollop to each potato and finish with the remaining scallions.
KaySo note: Do not overfill these. They are better as crisp little potato shells with a concentrated topping than as miniature twice-baked potatoes.
Bloody Mary Steak BitesOpen Recipe ↓
Serves3 people • appetizer portion
Total Time30 minutes
DifficultyModerate
EquipmentHeavy skillet or griddle; tongs.
Make-ahead: Make the horseradish sauce a day ahead. Cut and season the steak up to 2 hours ahead.

Ingredients

  • 12 oz sirloin steak, cut into 1-inch cubes
  • 1 tbsp neutral oil
  • 1 tsp Worcestershire sauce
  • 3/4 tsp celery salt
  • 1/2 tsp coarse black pepper
  • 1/4 tsp onion powder
  • Pinch of cayenne, optional
  • 1 tbsp butter
  • 1/3 cup sour cream
  • 2 tbsp mayonnaise
  • 1 1/2 tbsp prepared horseradish
  • 1 tsp Dijon mustard
  • 1 tsp Worcestershire sauce
  • Black pepper to taste

Step-by-step

  1. Mix sour cream, mayo, horseradish, Dijon, Worcestershire and pepper. Refrigerate while you cook the steak.
  2. Pat the steak cubes very dry. Toss with oil, Worcestershire, celery salt, black pepper, onion powder and cayenne.
  3. Heat a heavy skillet over high heat until very hot. Work in two batches if necessary so the steak sears instead of steaming.
  4. Add steak in a single layer and leave it alone for 60–90 seconds. Turn and cook another 60–90 seconds for medium-rare to medium, depending on cube size.
  5. Add butter for the final 30 seconds and toss the steak through it.
  6. Transfer immediately to a platter. Let rest 3 minutes, then serve with toothpicks and the cold horseradish sauce.
KaySo note: The Bloody Mary connection comes from celery salt, Worcestershire, pepper and horseradish—not tomato juice. That keeps this tasting like good steak.
